# Voice editing and bilingual localization

Read this before a full article or a substantial style revision. Edit the **whole piece**: a list of forbidden words cannot repair an article whose angle, pacing, and judgments feel manufactured.

## Choose the voice from the work

1. Identify the reader, what they already know, what decision or task brought them here, the publication's role, and the article's genre. A product announcement, tested comparison, practical guide, and opinionated analysis need different openings and levels of personality.
2. Use the target site's relevant articles as the primary voice reference. Note its point of view, level of technical detail, evidence habits, treatment of competitors, and how directly it speaks to readers. A homepage slogan is weak evidence for long-form prose.
3. If the site has little editorial history, examine a small set of **current, genre-matched** examples. Useful starting points include [OpenAI's blog](https://openai.com/news/), [Claude's blog](https://claude.com/blog), and [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/). Choose by article type: official product writing can model precise explanation and scope; practitioner essays can model a reader-facing problem, concrete examples, and honest judgment. Read actual articles before using them. Other strong, relevant writers may be better for the topic.
4. Record transferable moves in the brief: how the piece earns its opening, where it gives the answer, how it explains tradeoffs, how it uses examples, and how it closes. **Do not copy distinctive phrasing, metaphors, anecdotes, sentence patterns, or one author's full structure.** Reference articles inform craft, not facts about the subject; verify those separately.
5. If sources conflict with the target brand or the reader's needs, follow the brand and the task. Do not turn every article into an OpenAI announcement or a Lenny essay.

Examples to inspect when the genre fits: [OpenAI's Adoption channel introduction](https://openai.com/index/introducing-the-adoption-news-channel/) frames a changed reader problem before explaining its editorial promise; [Claude's Research announcement](https://claude.com/blog/research) connects a capability to concrete uses; [Lenny's Strategy Blocks guide](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/strategy-blocks-an-operators-guide) starts from an operator's problem and develops a usable method. These are craft references, not required sources, templates, or text to imitate. Prefer newer or closer examples when they fit the assignment better.

A useful one-line voice decision is: “For [reader] doing [job], sound [specific qualities]; use [kind of evidence]; avoid [genre mismatch].” Avoid numeric formality or energy scores.

## English: sound like a knowledgeable person

- Start where the reader's real problem or decision starts. A direct answer, concrete scene, surprising constraint, or useful distinction can work; choose the one the evidence supports. Do not open with a stock “Looking for …?” template or promise hands-on testing that did not happen.
- Make each section advance the article's argument. State what matters, show the evidence or example, explain the implication. Vary this rhythm when the content calls for it.
- Use plain, precise verbs and nouns. Prefer “The free tier limits exports” to “The robust solution seamlessly empowers workflows.” Avoid hype, empty intensifiers, generic transitions, and unearned superlatives.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length for meaning and pace. Short sentences can carry a conclusion; longer ones can hold a qualification. No fixed word or sentence-length targets.
- Use a conversational register only where it fits the publication. Contractions, first person, questions, humor, and strong judgments are tools, not requirements. Keep a sober tone for sensitive or technical claims.
- Attribute experience honestly. “We tested,” “in our workflow,” and “users report” require corresponding evidence. Distinguish reported facts, observed behavior, and editorial judgment.

## 中文：先准确，再地道，再有文采

“信”保留事实、条件、时间、程度、因果和不确定性；“达”让中文读者一遍读懂；“雅”是得体、有分寸，不是堆四字词或书面套话。

- 如果已有英文稿，先提取每段要解决的问题、事实和判断，再用中文重新组织。允许拆分、合并、调换句序，必要时换成中国读者熟悉的场景；不可改动证据所支持的结论。不要逐句对译。
- 如果从头写中文，直接用中文构思标题、段落和转折。优先使用该领域中文读者平时会说和会搜索的词。保留确有必要的英文产品名、术语或界面标签，并在第一次出现时解释。
- 用自然主谓结构和具体动作代替英语名词化、被动句和“进行……的实现”。少用“此外”“与此同时”“值得注意的是”“赋能”“生态闭环”等自动拼接式转场；需要转折时说清前后关系。
- 标题、导语、CTA 和例子按中文语境重写。英文的俏皮话、缩写和文化梗不必硬译。简体与繁体分别检查用词和字形；不要把机器转换当作本地化。
- 保持产品称谓、价格、货币、地区适用性和引用与来源一致。不能为“接地气”自行加入未验证的国内可用性、政策或使用体验。

**编辑示例，非可复用模板：**

- 生硬：“该工具使用户能够对他们的电子邮件工作流程进行优化。”
- 自然：“它能帮你把收件箱里的邮件分清轻重，先处理真正要紧的事。”
- 但只有证据显示它确实能区分邮件优先级时，才能用后一句；否则改写为已核实的功能。

## Full-piece editorial pass

After the factual draft, read the title, opening, section starts, transitions, and ending together, then read several body paragraphs aloud in the target language.

- Does the opening make a credible promise, and does the body deliver it?
- Is there a coherent progression, or are sections interchangeable list items?
- Where does the writer merely repeat features, and where do they help the reader decide or act?
- Are caveats placed next to the claims they qualify? Are comparisons fair and criteria explicit?
- Would a knowledgeable native reader naturally say these lines? Flag translated syntax, boilerplate, repetitive openings, and abrupt register shifts.
- Is the voice appropriate across the piece, including metadata and CTA? Cut anything that exists only to sound “SEO” or “AI optimized.”

Rewrite the weakest passages in context, then reread the joins. A word blacklist may help catch clichés, but never substitute it for editorial judgment. Run the factual and format checks in [quality checks](quality-checks.md) after substantive edits.
