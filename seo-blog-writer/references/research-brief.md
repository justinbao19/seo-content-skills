# Editorial research brief

The brief is a decision record, not a form with quotas. Keep it as short as the task permits. Save it at `{output_dir}/research-brief.md` for substantial articles or when requested; a short task may use an in-memory outline.

## Decide before drafting

1. **Reader and job:** Who is reading, what do they need to know or do, and what do they already understand?
2. **Search intent and promise:** Which query or cluster brings them here? What specific answer will the page deliver? What should remain outside scope?
3. **Original contribution:** What can this publication explain, compare, test, or synthesize that existing pages do not? Do not claim testing, firsthand use, expert input, or proprietary data without evidence.
4. **Evidence:** List the consequential claims and their best sources, including dates, limitations, and unresolved checks. Distinguish source facts from interpretation.
5. **Article arc:** Choose the opening, progression, useful examples, and ending for this subject. A comparison may need criteria and a table; a guide may need steps and failure cases; an analysis may need an argument and counterarguments. Add FAQ only when distinct reader questions remain unanswered in the body or the destination requires it.
6. **Language and voice:** Specify locale, target-site voice, genre-matched reference articles if useful, terms that sound native, and expressions to avoid. Draft Chinese directly from facts or plan a meaning-first localization from an English source. Follow [voice editing](voice-editing.md).
7. **Discoverability:** Propose a clear title/H1 and description that say what the page is about. Use a year only if freshness matters and the material is current. Plan internal links for relevant reader next steps and external citations for claims. No word-count multiplier, keyword density, or fixed link target.
8. **Destination:** Record the actual CMS/frontmatter schema, locale/slug conventions, image requirements, and review or publication boundary.

A compact brief can look like this:

```markdown
# Brief: [working title]
Reader and task: ...
Query and article promise: ...
Distinct value: ...
Evidence and open checks:
- [claim] — [source URL, date, limitation]
Shape: [opening] → [main reasoning/sections] → [reader decision or action]
Voice: [locale, register, reference moves, terms to use/avoid]
Useful links and assets: ...
Destination constraints: ...
```

The outline is provisional. Change it when research or the draft shows a better way to answer the reader. Do not pause for approval unless the user requested a review checkpoint.
