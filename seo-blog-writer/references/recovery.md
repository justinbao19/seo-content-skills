# Recovery when research or drafting is blocked

Use the evidence and tools actually available. Record the limitation and keep the resulting claims within what can be supported.

- **Target site cannot be read:** Try an available alternate page or search result, then use supplied product material. Do not infer current pricing or features from snippets. Ask for the missing fact only if it changes the article materially.
- **Search or competitor pages are blocked:** Work from the user’s stated intent, available results, and primary sources. Mark uninspected competitor details as unknown; do not imitate a result's structure from its snippet alone.
- **Voice examples are unavailable:** Use the target publication's accessible writing and the audience/genre. If neither is available, choose clear, restrained prose and flag the voice as provisional. Do not fill the gap with a generic “industry standard” template. For Chinese, favor plain native phrasing over translated corporate language.
- **A source does not support a claim:** Find a better source, narrow the wording to what is supported, or remove the claim. Do not replace missing evidence with “often,” “many,” or invented attribution.
- **A link cannot be opened:** Treat it as unverified, not broken. Search for a source you can inspect; otherwise disclose the limitation or remove the dependent claim.
- **Draft feels formulaic:** Diagnose the article-level cause: weak reader question, interchangeable sections, repetitive transitions, unsupported judgment, or translated syntax. Rework the outline or passage, then reread the surrounding text. Keyword-density tweaks and synonym swaps are not a repair.
- **Format or image gate fails:** Fix the actual path, field, asset, or WebP encoding and recheck that changed area. Do not call an unverified package publishable.

A shorter, well-supported article is preferable to a long draft padded with speculative facts or fixed SEO blocks. Deliver the useful part and state any remaining limitation.
