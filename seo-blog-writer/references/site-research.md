# Site and search research

Research enough to make editorial choices and support claims. The user’s brief, actual site, and project format rules take priority over generic SEO patterns.

## Learn the publication

Read provided product context first. Check the target site's relevant pages: product and pricing for factual scope, several related articles for editorial voice, and the content index or sitemap for existing coverage and internal links. Record what is verified, what is only inferred, and what cannot be reached. Missing pages do not justify inventing positioning or forcing a questionnaire; ask only when a missing fact materially changes the article.

For voice, look at **articles in the target language and genre**. Note how they address readers, explain concepts, use evidence, make recommendations, and treat competing products. If examples are scarce, use the genre-matched editorial sampling in [voice editing](voice-editing.md). For Chinese, prefer original Chinese writing in the subject area over translated marketing copy. For English, the OpenAI, Claude, and Lenny examples are starting points, not mandatory models or evidence for product claims.

## Understand the search need

Identify the likely query and the task behind it. Check live results where available to learn what readers expect, which questions remain poorly answered, and whether the query is primarily informational, comparative, transactional, or mixed. Search results are signals, not an outline to reproduce. Community discussions can reveal reader language and pain points; verify any factual claim separately.

Choose a primary phrase that describes the topic in the reader's own language. Related terms and questions are vocabulary to use when relevant, not an insertion checklist. Do not claim search volume, keyword difficulty, ranking probability, or competitor traffic without a measurement source. Do not infer that a result is easy to beat from its domain count or word count.

Review a few genuinely comparable pages when that helps locate a gap. Record what each does well, what readers still need, and what original value this publication can add. The best answer might be shorter, more specific, or differently structured. Do not default to a FAQ because a forum ranks.

## Output for the brief

Record:
- target reader, situation, and search task;
- the publication's legitimate perspective and any conflicts of interest;
- likely language and locale, with native terms readers use;
- verified product facts and high-impact claims with source URLs and dates;
- unanswered questions and facts that must be checked before publication;
- the distinct contribution of this article;
- relevant existing pages for natural internal links;
- voice references and the editorial moves worth borrowing, with no copied prose.

Continue from partial evidence when possible. State the limitation in the brief and keep unsupported details out of the draft.
