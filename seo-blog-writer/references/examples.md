# Editorial examples

These are illustrative decisions and original sample sentences, not templates or verified product facts.

## English comparison

**Reader:** A small team choosing an email tool after shared inbox handoffs became unreliable.

**Editorial choice:** Start with the decision that changes the recommendation. Explain the comparison criteria before naming winners. A table is useful if it lets the reader compare the same verified facts across tools; otherwise use prose. Acknowledge where the evidence cannot support a winner.

**Mechanical:** “Looking for the best email apps in 2026? We tested the top options, and these powerful tools will revolutionize your productivity.”

**More useful:** “A shared inbox solves a different problem from a faster personal inbox. If your team loses track of who owns a reply, start with assignment and handoff features. If the bottleneck is your own triage, prioritize keyboard flow and search.”

The second opening gives a decision rule without pretending testing occurred. Actual feature claims and recommendations would still need verification.

## English practical guide

**Reader:** Someone trying to keep important messages visible.

**Editorial choice:** Open with a concrete failure mode, give a workable first step, then explain where that approach breaks. Use a calm, helpful voice. Do not stretch the guide to match competitor length or insert a FAQ when the body resolves the questions.

**Possible opening:** “Start with the messages you cannot afford to miss: customers, your manager, and active projects. Route those into one review queue before you automate anything else. A filter that hides a critical thread is worse than no filter.”

This is guidance, not a claim about a named product.

## Chinese localization

**English idea:** “The fastest tool is not necessarily the best choice for a team if no one can see who owns the next reply.”

**Literal and stiff:** “最快的工具对于团队不一定是最佳选择，如果没有人能够看到谁拥有下一个回复。”

**Natural:** “团队选邮件工具，光看处理速度不够。要是连下一封邮件该由谁回复都说不清，回得再快也容易漏事。”

The Chinese version keeps the causal point while changing sentence order and phrasing. Confirm that the article's audience actually works with shared inboxes before using this example.
